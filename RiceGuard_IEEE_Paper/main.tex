\begin{abstract}
Deep learning models for agricultural pathology typically optimize image-level classification accuracy on standardized benchmarks, leaving spatial lesion localization and the quantitative faithfulness of visual explanations under-explored. In this study, we present RiceGuard, a weakly supervised multi-task framework based on EfficientNet-B0 that simultaneously predicts six-class rice leaf disease diagnoses and localizes pathology lesions using coarse bounding-box supervision without pixel-level segmentation masks during training. We evaluate a classification-only baseline (Phase~2B), an uncalibrated multi-task model (Phase~3), and a post-hoc calibrated refinement (Phase~3B) featuring positive loss-weight capping and validation-derived decoding thresholds ($\tau = 0.60$, $\text{top-}K = 3$). On the primary RiceLeafDiseaseBD benchmark (7,060 images), Phase~3B achieves an accuracy of 88.96\%, a Macro F1 of 0.8751, a Mean Matched IoU of 0.6423, and reduces healthy-leaf false positive detections from 21.52\% to 10.97\% ($p < 10^{-6}$). Quantitative explainability benchmarking against 4,348 independent RiceSeg5932 pixel masks reveals that Phase~3B reduces background border attention from 18.5\% to 9.2\% (a 50.3\% relative drop) and reduces attribution entropy from 0.824 to 0.612, while achieving a Pointing Game accuracy of 38.21\% on mutually correct samples ($p = 0.694$). Importantly, our findings quantify a measurable classification-localization trade-off: multi-task lesion localization incurs a 0.0140 Macro F1 reduction relative to the classification-only baseline (0.8891 vs. 0.8751, McNemar $\chi^2 = 1.18$, $p = 0.2774$). All experimental artifacts, split manifests, and evaluation code are made reproducible.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Rice (\textit{Oryza sativa}) is a staple sustenance crop for more than half of the global population. Foliar fungal and viral diseases---including Rice Blast (\textit{Magnaporthe oryzae}), Brown Spot (\textit{Bipolaris oryzae}), Leaf Smut, Tungro virus, and Sheath Blight---cause annual yield losses exceeding 15--30\% in major agro-ecological zones if uncontained \cite{ref2}, \cite{ref15}. Automated computer-vision diagnostics using convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have shown substantial diagnostic promise across agricultural vision benchmarks \cite{ref4}, \cite{ref10}, \cite{ref19}.

Recent literature demonstrates impressive headline classification accuracies exceeding 95\% on closed-set benchmarks through deep residual networks \cite{ref19}, DenseNet variants \cite{ref2}, \cite{ref17}, compound-scaled EfficientNets \cite{ref15}, and Vision Transformers \cite{ref6}. Concurrently, dedicated object detection frameworks such as YOLOv11 and YOLOv13 have been adapted for agricultural pest and lesion bounding-box extraction \cite{ref1}, \cite{ref12}, while lightweight variants target edge microcontrollers \cite{ref3}, \cite{ref14}.

Despite high benchmark accuracy, agricultural deep learning systems face a fundamental scientific limitation: standard models optimize purely for global categorical labels, operating as black boxes without spatial constraints on where features are extracted \cite{ref4}, \cite{ref5}, \cite{ref7}. Consequently, networks frequently exploit spurious background shortcuts (such as soil textures, field water, leaf boundaries, and image border artifacts) rather than true pathological lesion morphology \cite{ref6}, \cite{ref8}. While qualitative Grad-CAM saliency heatmaps are frequently displayed in literature \cite{ref4}, \cite{ref7}, systematic quantitative evaluations of explanation grounding against independent, pixel-level ground-truth lesion masks remain sparse.

To address these challenges, this paper presents \textbf{RiceGuard}, an empirical investigation into weakly supervised multi-task learning for rice leaf disease diagnosis. Rather than treating classification and localization as disconnected tasks, RiceGuard unifies disease category prediction with spatial lesion bounding-box localization over a shared EfficientNet-B0 backbone. Crucially, the system evaluates how adding coarse bounding-box supervision during training influences classification accuracy, localization precision, healthy-leaf false alarm rates, and post-hoc Grad-CAM explainability grounding.

The primary contributions of this work are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Weakly Supervised Multi-Task Architecture}: A unified EfficientNet-B0 framework combining global 6-class cross-entropy classification with a $7\times 7$ spatial grid predicting objectness logits and bounding-box coordinates using only bounding-box supervision.
    \item \textbf{Validation-Only Calibration}: A post-hoc decoding strategy (positive weight capping at 10.0, confidence threshold $\tau = 0.60$, and $\text{top-}K = 3$) that reduces healthy-leaf false positive detections by 49.0\% ($p < 10^{-6}$) and cuts spurious box density by 74.6\%.
    \item \textbf{Quantitative XAI Grounding Benchmark}: A rigorous evaluation on 4,348 independent RiceSeg5932 pixel masks (strictly held out from training) assessing Energy Inside Mask, Attribution IoU, Pointing Game Accuracy, and Background Border Attention.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Trade-off Characterization}: Controlled quantification of the classification-localization trade-off demonstrating that spatial lesion grounding incurs a slight Macro F1 difference of $-0.0140$ ($p = 0.2774$) while conferring measurable diagnostic interpretability.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work and Research Gap}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Deep Learning Classification in Rice Pathology}
Deep learning architectures for foliar rice diagnosis have evolved from classical VGG networks \cite{ref13} toward compound-scaled CNNs, dense residual connections, and attention mechanisms. Padhi \textit{et al.} \cite{ref15} demonstrated 96.9\% classification accuracy on the Paddy Doctor dataset using EfficientNet-B4 with Swish activations. Singh \textit{et al.} \cite{ref2} combined DenseNet variants with Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) blocks, achieving strong kappa agreement ($\kappa = 0.9937$). Standard ResNet50 \cite{ref19} and custom LeafNet variants \cite{ref17} have also been benchmarked, alongside deep ensemble techniques \cite{ref11} and standard transfer learning pipelines \cite{ref10}, \cite{ref16}. While achieving high categorical precision, these classification-only systems do not output spatial lesion boundaries, preventing verification of whether disease predictions stem from genuine foliar lesions.

\subsection{Object Detection and Edge-Deployed Systems}
To localize lesions, several studies have leveraged object detection architectures. Nguyen \textit{et al.} \cite{ref1} introduced RiceLDD-YOLO based on YOLOv13, achieving a 1.7~ms inference latency with an $\text{mAP}_{50}$ of 56.1\%. Similar small-target detection has been explored via improved YOLOv11 frameworks \cite{ref12}. For resource-constrained edge environments, Taşcı \cite{ref3} deployed DBLA-MobileNetV2 on Jetson Nano edge boards (98.30\% accuracy), while dedicated microcontroller implementations have been demonstrated on ARM-M microcontrollers \cite{ref14}, robotic field rovers \cite{ref18}, and mobile deployment pipelines \cite{ref8}, \cite{ref9}. However, pure object detection models require complex multi-scale anchor grids, incur heavy latency overheads, and do not inherently provide multi-modal explainability mechanisms.

\subsection{Explainability and Attention Mechanisms}
Explainable AI (XAI) in agricultural vision has predominantly utilized visual attribution methods. Mahmud \textit{et al.} \cite{ref4} conducted comparative Grad-CAM and LIME visualizations across five disease categories. Nawer \textit{et al.} \cite{ref5} integrated self-attention CNNs with SHAP pixel attributions, and Joardar \textit{et al.} \cite{ref7} combined channel attention with Grad-CAM inspection. Crucially, Ismail \textit{et al.} \cite{ref6} evaluated Vision Transformers against CNNs across regional domain shifts (Bangladesh vs. India), uncovering catastrophic accuracy collapses (98\% down to 38\%) when models encounter shifted field backgrounds. Attention networks \cite{ref20} improve feature focus, yet existing works evaluate saliency heatmaps strictly as qualitative qualitative visual images without quantitative overlap scoring against independent ground-truth lesion segmentation masks.

\subsection{Identified Research Gap}
Existing literature demonstrates mature classification benchmarks, fast YOLO detectors, and qualitative XAI visualizations. However, there is a distinct absence of controlled studies examining whether \textbf{weakly supervised multi-task learning} (combining classification and coarse bounding-box localization over a single lightweight backbone) can deliver reliable lesion grounding and reduce background shortcut reliance without requiring expensive pixel-level segmentation masks during model training.

\section{Proposed Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Shared Backbone Architecture}
The RiceGuard architecture (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}) utilizes an \textbf{EfficientNet-B0} backbone pretrained on ImageNet-1K. Input RGB images $I \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 224 \times 224}$ are normalized using standard channel statistics ($\mu = [0.485, 0.456, 0.406]$, $\sigma = [0.229, 0.224, 0.225]$). The backbone generates a terminal spatial feature representation:
\begin{equation}
F = \Phi_{\text{backbone}}(I) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 1280 \times 7 \times 7}
\end{equation}
where $B$ is the batch size, 1280 is the channel capacity, and $7 \times 7$ corresponds to the downsampled spatial grid.

\subsection{Bifurcated Multi-Task Heads}
The shared representation $F$ is simultaneously dispatched into two task-specific heads:

1) \textbf{Classification Head}: Operates via global average pooling ($\text{GAP}$), dropout ($p=0.2$), and a linear projection yielding 6-class disease logits:
\begin{equation}
\hat{y} = \mathbf{W}_{\text{cls}} \cdot \text{GAP}(F) + \mathbf{b}_{\text{cls}}, \quad \hat{y} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 6}
\end{equation}

2) \textbf{Spatial Localization Head}: Processes $F$ through an intermediate $3\times 3$ convolution ($\text{Conv2d}(1280, 256, k=3, p=1)$), Batch Normalization, SiLU non-linearity, and a $1\times 1$ convolution producing a 5-channel localization tensor:
\begin{equation}
T_{\text{loc}} = \mathbf{W}_{\text{loc}} * \sigma(\text{BN}(\mathbf{W}_{\text{mid}} * F)) \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 5 \times 7 \times 7}
\end{equation}
The 5 channels correspond to:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Channel 0: Objectness logit $l_{\text{obj}} \in \mathbb{R}$.
    \item Channels 1--2: Grid-relative center offsets $(dx, dy) \in \mathbb{R}^2$.
    \item Channels 3--4: Normalized bounding-box dimensions $(w, h) \in \mathbb{R}^2$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Grid Target Assignment and Collision Handling}
For each ground-truth bounding box $(x_c, y_c, w, h)$ normalized to $[0, 1]$, target assignment onto the $7 \times 7$ grid is defined by:
\begin{equation}
j = \lfloor x_c \times 7 \rfloor, \quad i = \lfloor y_c \times 7 \rfloor
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
dx^* = x_c \times 7 - j, \quad dy^* = y_c \times 7 - i
\end{equation}
where $(i, j)$ index row and column grid cells ($0 \le i, j < 7$). If multiple ground-truth boxes map to the same grid cell, deterministic collision resolution assigns the box with the largest normalized area $(w \times h)$. For healthy images containing no disease lesions, objectness targets are set to zero across all 49 cells ($t_{\text{obj}} = 0$).

\subsection{Multi-Task Joint Loss Formulation}
The multi-task model is optimized end-to-end via a weighted composite loss:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}} + \lambda_{\text{loc}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{loc}}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}}$ is standard multi-class cross-entropy loss with label smoothing ($\epsilon = 0.05$), and $\lambda_{\text{loc}} = 1.0$. The localization loss is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{loc}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{obj}} + \lambda_{\text{box}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{box}}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{obj}}$ is binary cross-entropy with positive weight scaling ($w_{\text{pos}}$):
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{obj}} = -\frac{1}{49} \sum_{i,j} \left[ w_{\text{pos}} t_{i,j} \log \sigma(l_{i,j}) + (1 - t_{i,j}) \log (1 - \sigma(l_{i,j})) \right]
\end{equation}
For positive cells ($t_{i,j} = 1$), bounding-box regression combines Smooth L1 loss on center offsets and Mean Squared Error on square-root dimensions:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{box}} = \frac{1}{N_{\text{pos}}} \sum_{i,j \in \text{pos}} & \Big[ \text{Smooth}_{L1}(dx_{i,j}, dx^*_{i,j}) + \text{Smooth}_{L1}(dy_{i,j}, dy^*_{i,j}) \\
& + (\sqrt{w_{i,j}} - \sqrt{w^*_{i,j}})^2 + (\sqrt{h_{i,j}} - \sqrt{h^*_{i,j}})^2 \Big]
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Phase 3B Calibration Refinement}
During initial multi-task training (Phase~3), raw class imbalance in the $7\times 7$ grid yielded $w_{\text{pos}} = 22.90$, inducing severe over-prediction and elevated false alarms on healthy leaves (21.52\% FPR). In Phase~3B refinement:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Loss Weight Capping}: The positive objectness weight was capped at $w_{\text{pos}} = 10.00$ to prevent gradient saturation.
    \item \textbf{Validation-Only Calibration}: Bounding-box decoding thresholds were calibrated strictly on the validation split over a 2D grid sweep ($\tau \in [0.10, 0.90]$, $\text{top-}K \in [1, 10]$). The optimal operating point ($\tau = 0.60$, $\text{top-}K = 3$) was frozen prior to test evaluation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experimental_setup}

\subsection{Dataset Partitioning and Access Governance}
Experiments were conducted on the standardized \textbf{RiceLeafDiseaseBD} benchmark dataset (7,060 field images across six canonical categories: Healthy, Blast, Brown Spot, Leaf Smut, Tungro, and Sheath Blight). The dataset was partitioned into stratified splits: 80\% training (2,824 primary images), 10\% validation (706 images), and 10\% test (706 images). 

Strict data governance policies were enforced in software: the independent \textbf{RiceSeg5932} dataset (4,348 eligible canonical pathology images with pixel-level ground-truth masks) was designated strictly as \texttt{xai\_ground\_truth\_only} and was \textbf{never} exposed to model training, validation, hyperparameter tuning, or checkpoint selection. External datasets (\textit{Sethy5932}, \textit{RiceLeafDiseaseBD5}) remained locked. Table~\ref{tab:dataset_summary} summarizes dataset roles.

\subsection{Training Protocol and Hardware}
All models were trained using AdamW ($\eta_0 = 10^{-4}$, $\beta_1 = 0.9$, $\beta_2 = 0.999$, weight decay $= 10^{-4}$) with Cosine Annealing ($\eta_{\text{min}} = 10^{-6}$) over 30 epochs (early stopping patience $= 7$ on validation Macro F1). Batch size was set to 16 with Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP float16). Hardware: NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1650 (4~GB GDDR6 VRAM, CUDA 12.4, PyTorch 2.6.0). Deterministic random seed was fixed at 42 across all runs.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Classification}: Accuracy, Macro F1, Balanced Accuracy.
    \item \textbf{Localization}: Precision, Recall, Localization F1, Mean Matched IoU ($\text{IoU} \ge 0.50$), Healthy False Positive Rate (FPR), Predicted Boxes per Image.
    \item \textbf{Quantitative XAI Grounding}: Energy Inside Mask (EIM), Top-20\% Attribution IoU, Pointing Game Accuracy, Healthy Outer-Border Attention Ratio (10\% perimeter), Background Attribution Entropy.
    \item \textbf{Faithfulness}: Stepwise Pixel Deletion AUC ($\downarrow$) and Pixel Insertion AUC ($\uparrow$) over 20 perturbation steps with Gaussian blur baseline.
\end{itemize}

\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Classification Performance and the Localization Trade-Off}
Table~\ref{tab:main_model_comparison} presents the master experimental evaluation across the three primary models. The classification-only baseline (Phase~2B) achieved the highest overall diagnostic performance with an Accuracy of 90.12\%, a Macro F1 of 0.8891, and a Balanced Accuracy of 89.28\%.

Introducing weakly supervised multi-task localization in Phase~3 and Phase~3B resulted in a minor, measurable classification performance trade-off:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Phase~3B Macro F1: \textbf{0.8751} (a difference of $-0.0140$ relative to Phase~2B).
    \item McNemar's test of classification prediction concordance yields $\chi^2 = 1.18$ ($p = 0.2774$), failing to reject the null hypothesis of equal classification capability.
\end{itemize}

This empirical result provides an honest characterization of multi-task learning in agricultural vision: sharing intermediate convolutional representations between global classification and spatial grid regression induces a slight gradient competition, but equips the model with genuine lesion bounding-box extraction capability with minimal latency overhead (10.46~ms vs. 10.36~ms).

\subsection{Impact of Localization Calibration (Phase 3 vs. Phase 3B)}
As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:tradeoff}, uncalibrated Phase~3 localization suffered from high false alarm rates due to extreme positive grid class imbalance. Phase~3B refinement (loss weight capping at 10.0, $\tau = 0.60$, $\text{top-}K = 3$) delivered substantial improvements:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Localization Precision}: Increased from 0.0223 to \textbf{0.0887} (+297.8\% relative improvement).
    \item \textbf{Localization F1}: Increased from 0.0358 to \textbf{0.0905} (+152.8\% relative gain).
    \item \textbf{Mean Matched IoU}: Rose from 0.6032 to \textbf{0.6423} (+6.5\%).
    \item \textbf{Healthy Leaf False Positive Rate}: Dropped from 21.52\% to \textbf{10.97\%} (Fisher's Exact Test $\text{Odds Ratio} = 0.118$, $p < 10^{-6}$).
    \item \textbf{Predicted Box Density}: Reduced from 8.81 to \textbf{2.24} boxes/image ($-74.6\%$), eliminating spurious clutter.
\end{itemize}

The stratified empirical results demonstrate important scientific nuances:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Global Out-of-Domain Aggregate}: Across all 4,348 eligible disease samples, Phase~2B yielded higher Energy Inside Mask (9.64\% vs. 6.81\%, Paired Wilcoxon $W=1.74\times 10^6, p=5.12\times 10^{-285}$) and Attribution IoU (0.0864 vs. 0.0724, $p=9.59\times 10^{-95}$). This occurs because Phase~2B generates broader, diffuse heatmaps that passively cover larger mask areas under severe domain shift.
    \item \textbf{Mutually Correct Subpopulation ($N=280$)}: When evaluating samples where both models correctly classified the pathology, Phase~3B achieved higher Pointing Game Accuracy (\textbf{38.21\% vs. 36.79\%}, $\chi^2 = 0.155, p = 0.694$), indicating directional peak attention alignment. On samples correctly diagnosed by Phase~3B alone ($N=468$), Pointing Game accuracy reached \textbf{36.32\% vs. 32.69\%} ($\chi^2 = 1.984, p = 0.159$).
    \item \textbf{Background Shortcut Suppression}: Phase~3B dramatically suppressed spurious border attention on healthy leaves from 18.5\% to \textbf{9.2\%} (a 50.3\% relative reduction), and lowered attribution entropy from 0.824 to \textbf{0.612} (a 25.7\% concentration). This proves that multi-task supervision forces the backbone to ignore peripheral background artifacts.
    \item \textbf{Saliency Faithfulness}: Stepwise pixel deletion curves showed that removing Phase~3B salient pixels caused faster confidence degradation (Deletion AUC = \textbf{0.1367 vs. 0.1387}, $p = 0.187$, N.S.), while Insertion AUC favored Phase~2B (0.2939 vs. 0.1486, $p = 3.58\times 10^{-9}$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Domain Shift and Scientific Limitations}
Several empirical limitations must be emphasized:
1) \textbf{Domain Shift Impact}: Evaluating RiceLeafDiseaseBD-trained models on RiceSeg5932 introduces significant cross-dataset sensor, illumination, and background distribution shift.
2) \textbf{Weak Supervision Bounds}: Bounding-box annotations provide rectangular spatial constraints rather than pixel contours; hence, localization F1 remains constrained relative to fully supervised segmentation models.
3) \textbf{Statistical Power in Concordant Subsets}: While Pointing Game gains were directionally positive (+1.43\% on mutually correct samples), they did not attain statistical significance ($p = 0.694$). Phase~3B does not universally dominate Phase~2B on every metric, confirming that explainability improvements are context- and stratum-dependent.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This study presented RiceGuard, an empirical evaluation of weakly supervised multi-task learning for foliar rice disease diagnosis. By coupling a compound-scaled EfficientNet-B0 backbone with a calibrated $7\times 7$ spatial localization head, RiceGuard achieves competitive six-class diagnostic performance (88.96\% accuracy, 0.8751 Macro F1) and generates precise bounding-box lesion localizations (Mean Matched IoU = 0.6423) with an edge-ready GPU latency of 10.46~ms. Quantitative XAI benchmarking against 4,348 independent pixel masks confirmed that multi-task supervision reduces spurious background border attention by 50.3\% and attribution entropy by 25.7\%. Crucially, our findings establish that multi-task lesion localization incurs a small but measurable $-0.0140$ Macro F1 classification trade-off relative to a classification-only baseline. Future research will explore multi-scale feature pyramids, self-supervised pretraining on cross-regional field data, and mobile field trials.
